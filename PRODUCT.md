# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Jan and the people he shares the link with (friends, family). Used both on the go in Barcelona to find a nearby spot, and at home on desktop browsing the full list before a trip. Mobile and desktop both matter.

## Product Purpose

A hand-picked, personal map of Barcelona spots (coffee, brunch, restaurants, and finer cuisine categories like sushi, tapas, vegan, bakery, pizza, burger, bar). Not a generic listings app: it's Jan's own curated taste, shareable as a single link. Success is someone opening the link, finding a place near them or filtering to what they're craving, and trusting the pick because it's personal, not crowdsourced.

## Brand Personality

Clean, minimal, dark. Confident and quiet rather than flashy. Feels like a well-kept personal notebook turned into a tool, not a startup product. No stock "app" chrome, no generic marketing gloss.

## Anti-references

Generic listing-app UI (Yelp/TripAdvisor card grids, star ratings, review counts). No SaaS dashboard aesthetic. No cream/beige "editorial" AI-default palette. Keep it dark and specific to this map, not templated.

## Design Principles

- Map and list are equally first-class: someone should be able to work entirely from the sidebar list or entirely from the pins.
- Category color-coding must stay legible and purposeful, not decorative — it's the primary way to scan fast.
- Personal curation over completeness: quality of note/context per place matters more than exhaustive metadata.
- Dark theme throughout; no light-mode toggle needed unless requested.
- Works properly on a phone screen — this is meant to be opened while walking around Barcelona.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard web accessibility: sufficient color contrast (dark theme, verify category colors against dark background), readable type sizes, no reliance on color alone (labels/text accompany every color-coded category). No specific WCAG level or assistive-tech requirement beyond that.
